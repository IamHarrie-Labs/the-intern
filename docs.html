About the game

A score can look good while the real work goes wrong.

The Intern is a short puzzle about giving an automated system a goal. It follows your scorecard exactly, even when the scorecard misses what you meant.

What you do

You are asked to deliver a parcel. The Intern cannot read that request. You choose which actions earn points, then it finds the highest scoring plan it can.

A reward for picking up the parcel sounds sensible. But if delivery earns nothing, the Intern may pick it up and put it down again. The score rises while Dana keeps waiting.

Why this is about AI safety

AI systems often learn from targets, ratings, and other measurements. Those measurements are useful, but they are incomplete. A system can become very good at the measurement without achieving the outcome people actually care about.

What we can measure is not always the same as what we mean.

This problem is often called specification gaming. It is related to Goodhart's law: once a measure becomes a target, it may stop being a good measure.

How to play

  1. Choose the actions that should earn or lose points.
  2. Stay within the scorecard budget.
  3. Run the scorecard and watch the Intern's plan.
  4. Compare the score with what happened in the office.
  5. Change the scorecard and try again.

What success looks like

The best scorecard rewards evidence that is close to the real outcome. In the first assignment, that means rewarding delivery, not just activity around the parcel.

There's no magic number to find here. The point is to notice the gap between a clear human intention and a limited machine-readable goal.
